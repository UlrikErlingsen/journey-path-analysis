# Sources and originality

## Public foundations

- De Weerdt, J., & Wynn, M. T. (2022). *Foundations of Process Event Data*. In *Process Mining Handbook*. <https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-08848-3_6>
- Shao, X., & Li, L. (2011). *Data-driven multi-touch attribution models*. KDD 2011. <https://doi.org/10.1145/2020408.2020453>
- Anderl, E., Becker, I., von Wangenheim, F., & Schumann, J. H. (2016). *Mapping the customer journey: Lessons learned from graph-based online attribution modeling*. International Journal of Research in Marketing, 33(3), 457–474. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijresmar.2016.03.001>

These sources establish the broad public domains of case-based event data, ordered touchpoint paths, transition models, and Markov-style removal calculations. Citation does not imply that the authors endorse this implementation.

## Independent implementation

All source code, validation rules, UI structure, evidence-pack design, fictional data generator, diagnostics, explanations, and decision boundaries were written independently for this repository. The product was designed from the declared user need and public literature.

It does not reproduce lecture slides, notes, cases, exercises, diagrams, assessment material, datasets, questionnaire wording, or institution-specific frameworks; general topics encountered in education only define the problem domain. General academic topics do not confer ownership over event logs, transition probabilities, Markov chains, confidence intervals, entropy, or bootstrap methods.

## Fictional data

The bundled example is deterministically generated by `src/tracesignal/examples.py`. Its customers, journeys, timestamps, touchpoints, subgroups, outcomes, and values are fictional. It is built to exercise the interface, not to resemble a real company or prove expected performance.

## Boundary against imitation

Trace Signal does not reproduce a branded course framework or a specific published tool interface. Its key product rule is that a journey visualization alone is insufficient: analysis is only available after a valid ordered event log is supplied, and the exported evidence consists of auditable tables and model diagnostics.
